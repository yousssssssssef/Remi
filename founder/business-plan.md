## The idea

- What it is: a kitchen companion that tracks the food in your home, plans and guides cooking, and builds the shopping list, as a phone app plus an optional countertop device.
- Who it is for: Arabic-speaking families of 3+ who cook at home; first segment Egyptian households in Egypt and the Gulf (the founder named MENA broadly; the pack narrowed it).
- What it sells, at what price: free app plus Remi Plus at $2.99 a month or $24.99 a year (founding price $19.99 a year for the first 1,000 households). The countertop device is parked until the app proves retention (set by /founder-pricing after the board).
- Where and how: online; the app ships on the App Store / Google Play, the device sold direct. Customer acquisition channel not decided.
- Budget and constraints: started as a university graduation project; no budget. Built by a student team; product largely built (app, backend, device prototype).

## Summary

**Verdict: Not yet.** The unit economics work, but demand doesn't: 1 of 20 simulated buyers said yes, and only to the free tier. The bar is 25%. What has to change is the effort of keeping the kitchen up to date, not the price. That's product work (helper mode and no-scan logging, see `offer.md`), then a real 30-household pilot (`launch.md`).

**What it is.** Remi is an Arabic and English kitchen app. Snap a supermarket receipt and Remi tracks what's in your kitchen, reminds you before food goes off, and suggests what to cook, with one shared list for the household. It's for Arabic-speaking families who cook at home, starting with Egyptian households in Egypt and the Gulf.

**The three numbers** (CFO, at $2.99 a month, founders unpaid):
- Each paying household leaves **$2.19 a month** (73%).
- Break-even is **50 paying households**.
- Year 1: **+$782**. That's only because the team is paid $0. One paid founder moves break-even to **507**.

**Where the board and the panel agree:**
- Households won't keep logging food. That's 12 of 20 panel "no"s, and the board's top risk.
- The device should wait.
- The market has to be narrowed.

**Where they differ:**
- The board saw a mild, possibly crowded market. The competitor map found a real but narrow gap: no Arabic app combines a pantry, expiry and receipt logging.
- The panel showed price isn't the main blocker (2 of 20 cited it). Trust and effort are.

**The biggest risk** is that talabat, instashop or Breadfast add a "your kitchen" tab filled from orders they already hold. The answer:
- Win one segment fast.
- Make logging take no effort: paste or forward an order.
- Consider partnering with a grocer instead of competing.

**What it needs to start:**
- About **$924** in cash.
- 30 pilot households for 4 weeks.
- A success line set in advance: at least 40% still logging in week 4, and at least 8 of 30 pay the founding price.

**This week:** scan 20 real Arabic receipts (Carrefour, Lulu, Hyper One, Spinneys), write down the match rate, and start recruiting the 30 pilot households.

## What the board said

**Vote: 3 fund if, 0 fund, 0 pass. Average score 4.3 / 10** (Offers 4, Monopoly 4, Product 5).

The board's view in one line: the product is far ahead of the business. There is a lot built and almost nothing proven about who pays, how they arrive, or whether they keep using it.

Memos: [offers](board/offers.md) · [monopoly](board/monopoly.md) · [product](board/product.md) · [brief](board/brief.md)

### Risks raised by more than one member

1. **Households may stop logging food.** (all three) Every feature runs on an accurate kitchen inventory. If households stop updating it after a few weeks, the suggestions go wrong, trust goes, and so do the lock-in and the data moat. Nobody has measured this yet.
2. **The device comes too early.** (all three) There is no unit cost, enclosure, certification or manufacturing plan, and no budget. Hardware would split a student team and eat cash before the app has shown that anyone stays.
3. **The market is too broad and there is no channel.** (Offers, Monopoly) "MENA households" lumps rich Gulf markets with poorer North African ones. No acquisition channel is chosen and the cost of getting a customer is unknown.
4. **Too many products, no one thing.** (Product, and Offers' "no named outcome") Inventory, planning, shopping, cook mode, nutrition, AI and a device. A buyer can't tell in five minutes which problem Remi solves for them.
5. **The category may be crowded, and the gains are small.** (Offers, Monopoly) Pantry, meal-planning and nutrition apps exist. The pain is real but mild, and Remi isn't yet ten times better at one thing. Not yet checked.

### Where they disagree

- **How much the build counts.** Product scores highest (5) because the craft, the bilingual catalogue and the closed loop are real assets. Offers and Monopoly score 4 because a built product without a proven buyer or channel isn't a business yet.
- **What the device is worth.** Monopoly sees it as the one thing an app competitor can't copy and as a brand asset. Offers and Product see it mainly as cost and risk. All three agree it should wait. They disagree on whether it is the long-term moat.
- **Where the moat is.** Monopoly puts it in the Arabic catalogue and receipt matching, growing from users' own scans. Offers puts the edge in the household as buyer. Product says neither matters until the loop survives real life.

### Conditions (checklist for the rest of the pack)

- [ ] **One segment, one country.** Name the beachhead (country and household type), size it, and show nobody already owns Arabic-first kitchen tracking there. → `/founder-competitors`, `/founder-consumer`
- [ ] **One thing, one outcome.** A one-sentence promise and one measurable result (money saved, food not wasted, or dinner with no effort). Cut or hold back what doesn't serve it, such as streaks, awards and recipe import. → `/founder-consumer`, `/founder-offer`
- [ ] **A real retention pilot.** Real households in the target segment use the app for 30 to 60 days. Set the target share still logging in advance, and show they get a useful moment in the first session. (A simulated panel can't prove this.) → `/founder-launch`
- [ ] **Near-effortless logging.** Keeping the kitchen accurate takes seconds a week. Receipt scanning and cook-deducts-ingredients are the base.
- [ ] **One channel, tested.** A small partner or paid test brings real households in, at a measured cost per household that the price can cover. → `/founder-marketing`, `/founder-cfo`
- [ ] **App first, device later.** Sell the subscription first. Don't spend on hardware or take pre-orders until retention and the channel are proven and the CFO has a device unit cost and price that work. → `/founder-cfo`, `/founder-pricing`
- [ ] **Know the running cost.** AI cost per active household per month (receipt reading, Ask Remi). → `/founder-cfo`
- [ ] **Offer before price.** Stack the existing features against the buyer's obstacles, with an affordable guarantee, real urgency (such as a founding-household price) and a name. Then price it against competitors, not by being cheaper. → `/founder-offer`, `/founder-pricing`
- [ ] **State the secret and the moat.** One line on what Remi knows about MENA kitchens that global apps get wrong, and how the Arabic catalogue grows harder to copy each month.

### The strongest version the board can see

This differs from the pitch. It is not a subscription-plus-device business across all of MENA. It is an **Arabic-first app for one segment in one country**: for example, Arabic-speaking families in one Gulf city who shop at chains whose receipts Remi already reads. The promise is narrow and measurable, such as "scan your receipt and never throw out food you forgot you had." The app is free to start, with a household subscription. The catalogue and receipt matching grow from every scan, which is the part a global app can't copy quickly. The device stays a prototype, a brand asset and a demo until real households show they keep the kitchen up to date. Only then does it become a premium tier, priced from a real unit cost. The product is already strong enough for this version. Everything still missing is evidence, not code.

### 5. The gap

**There is a gap, and it is narrow.** None of the apps we checked combines an Arabic interface, a kitchen inventory with expiry, and receipt-based logging matched to a local Arabic/English food catalogue.

- The global inventory apps don't offer Arabic: KitchenPal, Samsung Food, NoWaste, Paprika and MyFitnessPal. Their UAE review counts are in single or low double digits.
- The Arabic-first apps are recipe apps with no inventory: Cookpad, Sayidaty, Wasfati.
- The one Arabic-localised planner with a pantry (Meal Plan & Weekly Menu) is a 50-language generic app with no receipt scanning.
- Users' second-biggest complaint is that logging is wrong or slow: products aren't found, expiry dates are wrong, items land in the wrong place. Remi's receipt matching against 851 foods and 2,074 Arabic/English aliases answers that complaint directly, if it is accurate on Gulf or Egyptian receipts.
- The biggest complaint is reliability: lost lists and broken sync. A boringly reliable shared household kitchen is also an opening.

What the gap is **not**: recipes, meal plans or calorie counting. Those are crowded and cheap ($2.99/mo), and in Arabic they are free.

One caution. The gap could be empty because demand is weak. No app here shows that MENA households keep logging food. The global apps' tiny UAE rating counts fit that reading as well as they fit "nobody has served them yet". The retention pilot from the board's checklist is still the test.

### 6. The threat: who could copy this fastest

1. **talabat, including instashop, and Breadfast. This is the biggest threat.** They already hold every grocery order line by line, in Arabic and English, for hundreds of thousands of households: talabat has 418K UAE ratings and Breadfast 159K in Egypt. They could add a "your kitchen" tab, populated automatically with no receipt to scan, plus a one-tap reorder of what is running out. That would beat Remi's logging on effort, and they have the distribution Remi lacks. Careem (164K ratings) and noon could do the same. Their incentive is reorders, not cutting waste, but the feature is the same.
2. **Samsung Food.** It already has pantry, meal plans, a shopping list and nutrition, and it ships on Family Hub fridges sold in the UAE. Adding Arabic and a regional catalogue would be a localisation project, not new product work.
3. **The developer of Meal Plan & Weekly Menu.** It already ships Arabic and a pantry with expiry, and it could add receipt OCR.

Remi's defence is what is hard to copy. That means a catalogue and receipt matcher tuned to local chains' receipts, which improves with every scan. It also means possibly partnering with a grocer instead of competing with one, for example taking order data from a retailer that lacks this feature.

### Routines (one page each)

1. **A receipt that didn't match.** Open the unmatched-lines report. For each line, add an alias (Arabic and English) or a product. Re-run the match on the stored lines. Note the shop name and add it to the shop list.
2. **A complaint (store review or email).** Reply within 24 hours in the language they used. Say what happened in one line and what you'll do. Never ask for a better rating. Log it in the risk register if it repeats.
3. **A release.** Run the tests (157 backend, 441 app). Do the phone smoke test (docs/phone-testing.md): sign up, scan a receipt, cook a meal, delete an account. Use a staged rollout at 10% and watch crashes for 24 hours.
4. **Weekly numbers.** Use the four numbers in `launch.md`. If week-4 logging retention falls below 25%, stop marketing and fix effort first.
5. **Data deletion request.** Deletion runs from Settings. If the request comes by email, verify it's the account owner, delete, and confirm in writing.

### Licences, permits, compliance (check each with the authority; not legal advice)

- **Company or sole-trader registration** in the founders' country, so you can receive store payouts. Apple and Google need a legal entity or individual with tax details.
- **Tax on digital services**: Apple and Google collect and remit VAT in most MENA markets they sell in, including the UAE, Saudi Arabia and Egypt. Confirm for each country.
- **Data protection**: Egypt's Personal Data Protection Law (Law 151 of 2020), the UAE's PDPL (Federal Decree-Law 45 of 2021), and Saudi Arabia's PDPL (SDAIA). The privacy policy and where data is stored have to match. Receipt photos are personal data.
- **Health and nutrition claims**: don't make medical or weight-loss claims. Allergen tags need expert review before they're shown as safe.

## Launch plan

**Bottom line: test with 30 real households before launching to the public.** The simulated panel can't answer the one question that matters: do real households keep their kitchen up to date after four weeks? A paid founding-household offer answers whether they'll pay.
